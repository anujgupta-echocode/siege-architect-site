Privacy Policy — Siege Architect

Effective date: 4 September 2026

Applies to: the Android application Siege Architect (com.anujgupta017.siegearchitect)

Published by: Anuj Gupta · Contact: anujgupta017@gmail.com

The short version. Siege Architect is free and is paid for by advertising. We collect nothing about you: there is no account, no login, no analytics of ours, and everything the game remembers — your progress, coins and blueprints — is stored on your own phone and never leaves it.

The one thing that does leave your phone is the advertising request. The app shows ads supplied by Google AdMob, and to select and measure those ads Google receives your device's advertising identifier and some technical information about the device. That is described in full below, together with how to turn personalised advertising off or reset the identifier. The browser version of the game shows no ads at all.

What we collect

Nothing.

We do not collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, files, precise location, or any usage or diagnostic telemetry. There is no sign-up, no login and no user profile. We operate no server; there is no database anywhere with a record of you in it.

“We” means Anuj Gupta, the developer. Google's collection as our advertising partner is a separate matter and is set out in the next section.

Advertising

The Android app includes the Google AdMob advertising SDK (part of Google Mobile Ads). It shows two kinds of advert:

  • a rewarded video you may choose to watch to double the coins from a battle — it is always optional, and declining it costs you nothing but the extra coins;
  • a full-screen advert between battles, shown at most once every third battle and never during play.

Nothing in the game is locked behind watching an advert.

What Google receives. When the app requests an advert, Google receives information needed to select, deliver, measure and secure that advert. This typically includes:

  • your device's Android advertising ID (a resettable identifier, not your name);
  • device and technical information — device model, operating-system version, language, screen size, network type, and the IP address the request comes from (from which an approximate, city-level location can be inferred);
  • ad interaction data — that an advert was requested, shown, watched to the end, or clicked.

Google uses this to serve adverts (personalised ones where permitted), to measure them, to detect invalid traffic and fraud, and for its own analytics. Google acts as an independent controller of that data, and its use of it is governed by Google's policies, not by ours:

  • Google Privacy Policy — policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services — policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • Google advertising technologies — policies.google.com/technologies/ads

What we receive back. Aggregate, anonymous performance figures in the AdMob dashboard — impression counts, revenue, fill rates by country. Nothing that identifies a person and nothing we could link back to a device or a player.

How to opt out or limit this

  • Turn off personalised advertising, or delete the advertising ID entirely: on your phone, open Settings → Privacy → Ads (on some devices Settings → Google → Ads), then use “Delete advertising ID” or “Opt out of Ads Personalisation”. Deleting the ID stops apps, including this one, from receiving it at all; you will still see adverts, but they will not be personalised.
  • Google's own ad settings, across Google services: adssettings.google.com
  • Play the browser version instead. The web build of Siege Architect contains no advertising SDK, makes no ad requests, and shows no adverts.
  • If you are in the EEA or the UK and the app shows you a consent form from Google, the choice you make there governs whether the adverts you see are personalised.

In-app purchases

There are none today. A one-off “remove ads” purchase may be added in a future version. If it is, this policy will be updated before that version is published; the purchase itself would be handled entirely by Google Play, and we would never see or handle your payment details.

What the app stores on your device

So the game can remember where you got to, it saves a single record in the app's own private storage — the WebView localStorage area inside the app's Android data directory, under the key siege_save_v1. That record contains:

  • which campaign levels you have completed and the stars you earned on them,
  • your in-game coin balance,
  • the castle blueprints you have designed and named,
  • your sound setting (a single on/off toggle).

If a “remove ads” purchase is ever added, a second key, siege_ads_removed, records that you bought it.

This information stays on your device. It is readable only by Siege Architect itself, it is never uploaded anywhere, and no one else — including us — can see it. The Google Mobile Ads SDK also keeps its own small store of technical values on the device; that is Google's, and clearing the app's data removes it too.

How to delete it. Use the reset option inside the game's Settings screen, or clear the app's storage from Android's Settings → Apps → Siege Architect → Storage → Clear data, or simply uninstall the app. Any of these permanently erases the saved record. There is nothing stored on a server of ours to delete on your behalf, and therefore no data-deletion request process for our own data — because we hold none. To remove what Google holds, use the advertising-ID controls described above and Google's own tools.

Network use

The game itself works offline: its code, artwork and sound are generated inside the app and packaged into the installed application, and a battle needs no network connection. The only network traffic the app generates is the advertising traffic described above. With the device in aeroplane mode, the game still plays — it simply shows no adverts.

The Android package declares the INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE permissions (needed to fetch adverts) and com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID (required by Android 13 and later for an app that reads the advertising identifier).

Analytics

We use no analytics product of our own — no Firebase Analytics, no crash reporting, no attribution or measurement SDK. The only measurement that happens is Google's own, inside the advertising system described above.

Children's privacy

Siege Architect is directed at players aged 13 and over and is not designed for children. Its Google Play target-audience declaration is 13+ and it is not part of the Designed for Families programme. The adverts it shows are served for a general audience and are not configured for child-directed treatment, which is precisely why the app is not offered to under-13s.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone of any age, because we collect none. If you believe a child under 13 has used the app and you want the advertising identifier associated with that device dealt with, delete it in Android's ad settings as described above, and contact us at anujgupta017@gmail.com with any question.

Third parties

We share nothing with third parties ourselves, because we hold nothing. The app contains exactly one third-party service that receives data: Google AdMob, as described in Advertising above. There is no other SDK, network, or partner in the build.

If you install the app from Google Play, Google's handling of your download and of the account you use with Play is governed by Google's privacy policy, not this one. That relationship is between you and Google, and we receive no personal data from it beyond the anonymous, aggregated install and crash statistics that Google shows to every developer in the Play Console.

Your rights

Data protection laws such as the GDPR and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act give you rights to access, correct, delete and port your personal data, and to object to its processing.

We hold no personal data about you on any server and identify no individual, so there is nothing for us to produce, correct, export or erase. You remain in full control of the only data we are involved with: the save file on your own device, which you can delete at any time, and the advertising identifier, which you can reset or delete in Android's settings.

Rights against Google in respect of the advertising data it collects are exercised through Google, using the links and settings above.

Security

The saved game is held inside the app's private application directory, which Android isolates from other apps. Advertising requests are sent over HTTPS. We operate no server, so there is no server-side store of ours that could be breached.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Siege Architect changes what it stores, adds a purchase, or adds another network service, this policy will be updated before that version is published, and the effective date at the top of this page will change. Material changes will also be described in the app's release notes on Google Play.
